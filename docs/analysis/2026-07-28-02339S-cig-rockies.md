**Read the two directional figures side by side and the structure is immediate.** The
fragility-weighted quantity that would have to *buy* under stress is 56% of open interest;
the quantity that would have to sell is 6%. This is a 9-to-1 asymmetry in the opposite
direction from the sell-side leader, and it is what a squeezable market looks like in these
units.

Note what a single combined figure would have done. `Q_sell + Q_buy = 20,368` — 62% of open
interest, a large-sounding number describing an event in which both sides liquidate into
each other, which cannot happen. The whole informational content here is in the split.

**Which term dominates: depth, overwhelmingly.** The trader count was 4 for the entire
window until the final week, so `ΔN = 0` and the breadth term is identically zero in every
week it can be computed. The whole build from 18,612 to 27,114 and the whole 9,809-contract

leaving.

Three of the four gaps in the trader column (05-12, 06-09, 06-16) are CFTC suppressing the
count because too few traders would be individually identifiable — which is itself
information about how few there are. Those weeks return null rather than an imputed count,
because an imputed trader count would flow straight into the average position that the
quadrant is read from.

The final week is the only breadth-dominated one: a fifth trader appeared, and the average
position per trader fell from 4,342 to 3,616. On the spec's quadrant that is
`wide_and_shallow`, but on a base of four traders it is one entity joining.

**This is as close to "narrow and deep" as the Disaggregated report can show.** A category
position equal to 55% of open interest, held by four to five traders, at an average of 3,616
contracts each — 11% of the entire market's open interest per trader.

## 5. Reading

Five traders are short 18,080 contracts, 55% of the open interest in this market, against a
physical hedger who is net long 18,649 at a fragility weight of 0.1. `Q_buy` is 9.4x
`Q_sell`.

**The fragile direction is up.** Everything that could be forced to transact in size here
must *buy*, and the natural seller is a hedging entity with a cash position behind it that
does not have to sell anything. The Producer/Merchant long is the largest gross position in
the market at 40,983 contracts, and at weight 0.1 it contributes 1,865 weighted contracts of
potential selling — roughly a tenth of the weighted buying it would face.

**What would have to happen to force that side out.** In principle: a price move against
them, a volatility increase that shrinks a vol-targeted book, or a margin change. This
document has none of those inputs, so it cannot name a level. But this market gives
something the sell-side market does not — **a recent, observed instance of exactly the event
in question.** On 2026-07-07 this same category covered 9,809 contracts in one week, 36% of
its position, with open interest falling 29%. Whatever the trigger was, the response was
fast and large, and it was four traders doing it.

That is the reason to take the current configuration seriously despite its small absolute
size. The concentration is extreme, the fuel is measurable (18,080 contracts of shorts that
must eventually be bought back or delivered against), and the market has demonstrated within
the last four weeks that a third of that position can move in a single reporting period. A
squeeze here does not require a crowd to panic; it requires one or two of five entities to
change their minds.

**The counterweight, and it is substantial.** The position is a third smaller than it was on
June 30 and has been rebuilt only slowly since the cover. The most recent week added a
trader and reduced the average size. And this is a 32,591-contract market — small in
absolute terms, which is why `Q_buy/OI` can reach 0.56 at all. Whether 18,400 weighted
contracts is a lot depends entirely on the daily volume in CIG Rockies basis, which this
package cannot see.

**What is missing here specifically:** no volume, so the central question — can five traders
buy back 18,080 contracts without moving the basis, and over how many days — is unanswerable
and `days_to_liquidate` is `None`; no price, so no squeeze level; and the reading leans hard
on the 1.0 weight assigned to Managed Money and the 0.1 assigned to Producer/Merchant, which
are configured judgements. Four of the five trader-count observations that would sharpen the
concentration picture are suppressed by CFTC.

## Bottom line

This market inverts the structure the design doc's worked example uses: the physical hedger
is long and the levered money is short, so the squeezable side is the short side.
Fragility-weighted buying pressure is 56% of open interest against 6% of selling pressure, a
9-to-1 asymmetry, and 73% of the fragility sits with **five traders**.

The concentration is the real finding, more than the ratio. Four traders built and then
partly dismantled this position over twelve weeks with no change in participant count at
all, and on 2026-07-07 they covered 9,809 contracts — a third of the book — in one week
while open interest fell 29%. That week is also the cleanest demonstration available of why
the flow decomposition is worth having: net position moved +9,809 and looked exactly like
aggressive buying, and it was a short position being closed, with a fuel supply that was
finite, published, and visible beforehand.

**In plain terms: a genuinely concentrated, genuinely squeezable short, in a small market,
which has already shown once this month that it can move a third of itself in a week.** That
is a real structural finding rather than a marginal one. It is still not a prediction: there
is no price here, no volume, and therefore no answer to whether a squeeze is close or what
it would cost — only a clear statement of which side would be the one running.
